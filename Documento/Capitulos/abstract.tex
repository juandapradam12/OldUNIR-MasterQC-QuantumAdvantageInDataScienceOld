\chapter{Abstract}

Quantum computing represents an emerging paradigm with transformative potential for data science, yet a significant gap persists between the theoretical promises of quantum algorithms and their practical applicability on real-world data. This work presents a systematic empirical comparative study of quantum versus classical algorithms across nine use cases covering supervised classification (QSVM), regression (QK-SVR), combinatorial optimisation (QAOA, VQE, fair closed TSP), one-class anomaly detection (OCSVM-ZZ) and one-step lag forecasting. Experiments use only real data from SKG Tecnología (mobility incidents and travel times in Bogotá, used as practice) and public reference benchmarks (Telco, Friedman~\#1, moons, blobs, sine, portfolio, TSP, MDS); no data are generated to suit the quantum kernel, because labelling them with the observable of the very feature map under test would make the comparison circular.

Results show that \textbf{no quantum advantage is observed} in any of the cases evaluated on real or reference data. In classification and regression the classical algorithms are superior (QSVM: $-4.5$~pp on SKG and $-3.8$~pp on Telco; QK-SVR: $-56.2$ and $-92.8$ points of $\Delta R^2\times100$ on SKG and Friedman). In combinatorial optimisation no advantage is observed either: QAOA matches the exact optimum in the assignment case (Use Case~3); in the portfolio case (Use Case~4) it reaches the exact optimum in only 6 of 10 seeds (VQE: 3 of 10) whereas the greedy baseline always finds it; and in closed TSP (Use Case~5) QAOA ties the exact optimum with NN+2-opt on Colombia $n=4$ (1867.6~km) under a fair protocol ---modelling value, not speedup. Use Case~7 illustrates the hybrid QAOA--COBYLA paradigm on the minimum dominating set: with $p=3$ the optimum of the 6-node network is recovered by sampling (mean probability of measuring it $5.7\%$, best restart $68.8\%$), tying exact ILP, but only 2 of 5 Erd\H{o}s--R\'enyi instances ($n=6$--$15$) yield a minimum dominating set against 5 of 5 for ILP and Greedy ($-60$~pp), with no measured advantage. Use Cases~8 and~9 confirm the pattern: OCSVM-ZZ and QK-SVR stay below the best classical method on SKG, Telco, moons, blobs and sine.

Two original contributions are presented. The first is the \emph{quantumness score} $\mathcal{Q}(D)$ (and its hardened revision $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rob}}$ in QML~v2), a vector of a-priori indicators that on real data show no quantum structure (null control $\approx+0.03\pm0.02$) and is presented as a diagnostic proposal, not a validated predictor. The second is the \emph{QCR Framework}, \textbf{formally posed} (protocol + open conjecture): Notebook~09 reported Telco lifts \emph{vs.\ RBF} via re-uploading ($\texttt{reps}=3$); under $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rob}}$ Telco stays \texttt{classical}, and on SKG rotation only changes the geometry verdict to \texttt{try-ZZ} without QSVM accuracy lift vs.\ GB/RF --- no quantum structure is observed in SKG/Telco (Section~\ref{sec:qml_v2_revision}). Strong validation is post-thesis work. Practical conclusion: adopt quantum algorithms as \emph{selective, complementary tools}. Experiments use Qiskit~2.x (2.3.0 and 2.6.1; see Appendix~\ref{anx:entorno}) on exact statevector simulation.
