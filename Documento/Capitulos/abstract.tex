\chapter{Abstract}

Quantum computing represents an emerging paradigm with transformative potential for data science, yet a significant gap persists between the theoretical promises of quantum algorithms and their practical applicability on real-world data. This work presents a systematic empirical comparative study of quantum versus classical algorithms across nine use cases covering supervised classification (QSVM), regression (QK-SVR), combinatorial optimisation (QAOA, VQE, fair closed TSP), one-class anomaly detection (OCSVM-ZZ) and one-step lag forecasting. Experiments use three dataset types per case when applicable: map-aligned native data (empirically validated map: \texttt{ZZFeatureMap}, not claimed exclusive), practice data from SKG Tecnología and public benchmarks.

Results show that quantum advantage is \emph{real but conditional}: it appears when the \textbf{feature map} (ZZ or another) is aligned with the data generator; ZZ is the map validated here. QSVM achieves $+38.3$~pp on map-aligned native data; classical methods win on real data without that alignment. QAOA cuts assignment cost by $-30.3\%$ vs greedy; Use Case~5 (closed TSP) shows QAOA tying the exact optimum with NN+2-opt on Colombia $n=4$ (1867.6~km) under a fair protocol ---modelling value, not speedup. Use Cases~8 and~9 confirm the same pattern: OCSVM-ZZ / QK-SVR win only on map-aligned novelty/targets (\texttt{zz\_phase}, \texttt{zz\_lag}); on moons/sine/SKG, classical wins.

Two original contributions are presented. The first is the \emph{quantumness score} $\mathcal{Q}(D)$ (and its hardened revision $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rob}}$ in QML~v2). The second is the \emph{QCR Framework}, \textbf{formally posed} (protocol + open conjecture): Notebook~09 reported Telco lifts \emph{vs.\ RBF} via re-uploading ($\texttt{reps}=3$); under $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rob}}$ Telco stays \texttt{classical}, and on SKG rotation only changes the geometry verdict to \texttt{try-ZZ} without QSVM accuracy lift vs.\ GB/RF --- SKG/Telco are \textbf{not} quantum-native (Section~\ref{sec:qml_v2_revision}). Strong validation is post-thesis work. Practical conclusion: adopt quantum algorithms as \emph{selective, complementary tools}. Experiments use Qiskit~2.3.0 on exact statevector simulation.
