\chapter{Abstract}

Quantum computing represents an emerging paradigm with transformative potential for data science, yet a significant gap persists between the theoretical promises of quantum algorithms and their practical applicability on real-world data. This work presents a systematic empirical comparative study of quantum versus classical algorithms across seven use cases covering three problem families: supervised classification (QSVM with quantum kernel), regression (QK-SVR) and combinatorial optimisation (QAOA, VQE, SA-QUBO). Experiments are conducted on three dataset types per case: data with native quantum structure, real data from SKG Tecnología (mobility incidents and travel times in Bogotá) and a public-domain benchmark dataset.

Results show that quantum advantage is \emph{real but conditional}: QSVM achieves $+38.3$~pp over quantum-native data, while classical algorithms outperform in classification and regression on real-world data. In combinatorial optimisation, QAOA achieves a $-30.3\%$ cost reduction in resource assignment against greedy heuristics. Use Case~7 illustrates the \emph{hybrid quantum-classical paradigm} applied to the NP-hard minimum dominating set problem: the QAOA-COBYLA loop, with a $2p$-parameter interface between quantum and classical modules, recovers the optimum with probability $\approx 73\%$ and establishes a modelling pattern generalisable to any QUBO-formulated problem.

Two original contributions are presented. The first is the \emph{quantumness score} $\mathcal{Q}(D)$, a composite metric based on kernel alignment ($\Delta A$), Frobenius distance ($d_F$) and differential spectral entropy ($\Delta H$), achieving a Spearman correlation of $\rho_S = 0.833$ ($p = 0.005$, $n = 9$) with empirical QSVM advantage. The second is the \emph{QCR Framework} (Quantum-Compatible Representation), a four-step protocol that automatically selects the optimal pre-mapping $T^*$: on Telco Churn it raises $\mathcal{Q}(D)$ from $-0.228$ to $+0.167$ and empirical advantage from $0$~pp to $+4.2$~pp via data re-uploading with \texttt{reps=3}. The practical conclusion is that quantum algorithms should be adopted as \emph{selective, complementary tools} within the enterprise technology stack — not as replacements for classical methods — being particularly promising for combinatorial optimisation with QUBO formulation. All experiments are conducted with Qiskit~2.4.1 on exact statevector simulation.
