\chapter{Abstract}

Quantum computing represents an emerging paradigm with transformative potential for data science, yet a significant gap persists between the theoretical promises of quantum algorithms and their practical applicability on real-world data. This work presents a systematic empirical comparative study of quantum versus classical algorithms across eight use cases covering supervised classification (QSVM with quantum kernel), regression (QK-SVR), combinatorial optimisation (QAOA, VQE, SA-QUBO) and one-class anomaly detection (OCSVM-ZZ). Experiments are conducted on three dataset types per case when applicable: data with native quantum structure (labels aligned with a feature map; the map empirically validated here is \texttt{ZZFeatureMap}, not claimed to be the only admissible one), practice data from SKG Tecnología (mobility incidents and travel times in Bogotá) and public-domain benchmarks.

Results show that quantum advantage is \emph{real but conditional}: it appears when the \textbf{feature map} (ZZ or another) is aligned with the data generator; ZZ is the map validated empirically in this thesis. QSVM achieves $+38.3$~pp on map-aligned native data, while classical algorithms outperform in classification and regression on real-world data without that alignment. In combinatorial optimisation, QAOA achieves a $-30.3\%$ cost reduction in resource assignment against greedy heuristics. Use Case~7 illustrates the \emph{hybrid quantum-classical paradigm} applied to the NP-hard minimum dominating set problem: the QAOA-COBYLA loop recovers the optimum with probability $\approx 73\%$. Use Case~8 (one-class anomalies, \emph{not} fraud) shows OCSVM-ZZ superior when novelty is aligned with that map (\texttt{zz\_phase}: AUC $0.864$ vs IsolationForest $0.553$); on moons/blobs and injected far outliers, IsolationForest wins.

Two original contributions are presented. The first is the \emph{quantumness score} $\mathcal{Q}(D)$ (and its hardened revision $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rob}}$ in QML~v2). The second is the \emph{QCR Framework}: Notebook~09 reported Telco gains via re-uploading ($\texttt{reps}=3$); under robust $\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{rob}}$ Telco stays classical and the observable rescue is \textbf{rotation} on SKG (\texttt{try-ZZ} verdict, without guaranteed QSVM accuracy lift --- Section~\ref{sec:qml_v2_revision}). The practical conclusion stands: adopt quantum algorithms as \emph{selective, complementary tools}. Experiments use Qiskit~2.3.0 on exact statevector simulation.
